# Data landscape for the dark-vessel pipeline (Southeast Asia, focus Vietnam)

> **"Dark" does not mean illegal.** It only means that no AIS position was matched to a radar detection. Many vessels are not required to carry AIS, AIS can be off for lawful reasons, and satellite AIS misses messages in busy coastal waters. Treat every unmatched detection as a lead for review, not as evidence of wrongdoing.

Date: 2026-10-02 (UTC). Companion file: `data/data_sources.csv` (one row per source, same columns as the tables plus `id` and `section`).

**Framing.** The project builds a sovereign, sensor-agnostic pipeline with its own detector. It does not rebuild Global Fishing Watch (GFW). Three reasons, each checked below: GFW data and APIs are noncommercial (GFW's own repo licence, read); GFW SAR detections lag real time by about 5 days (GFW client README, read) while the AWS Sentinel-1 mirror delivered scenes with a measured median of 3.5 h from sensing stop to upload; and Sentinel-1A delivered its last products on 29 June 2026 (last day present in the AWS bucket; the ASF documentation gives the same stop date; official end of operations reported as 30 June, pages not opened), so the constellation is now Sentinel-1C and 1D.

**Scope note.** `docs/STATUS.md` (2026-10-02) widens the AOI from Ca Mau to the whole South China Sea (Natural Earth marine areas, 3.58 million km2) and keeps Ca Mau as the detail sub-area. This document keeps the Vietnam focus (Ca Mau and Gulf of Tonkin) as briefed and adds the wider AOI wherever a figure could be computed from `data/aoi.gpkg` (read only).

## Summary

1. **Usable now, and clean for commercial use.** Sentinel-1 GRD from the AWS Open Data mirror (anonymous reads, S1C and S1D present, median 3.5 h from sensing stop to upload) with CDSE as the official fallback (free tier of 12 TB per 30 days). Copernicus terms allow commercial use (CDSE documentation). Add Sentinel-2 and Landsat for confirmation, ESA WorldCover for the sea mask, Satlas offshore-structure points (ODC-BY) for fixed objects, and the AI2 Skylight labels and weights (Apache-2.0) for training. All VERIFIED.
2. **Over the two Vietnamese sub-areas only Sentinel-1D delivers.** Ca Mau: 50 IW scenes in 90 days, all S1D. Gulf of Tonkin: 78 scenes touch the box, 67 S1D and 11 S1C, but every S1C scene overlaps at most 0.3% of the box. S1C does image elsewhere (433 IW products worldwide on 2026-09-28; 207 inside the wider South China Sea AOI in 90 days according to `docs/scs_regional.md`). The Sentinel-1A to 1C/1D transfer study must therefore take its 1C test scenes from the wider AOI or from other regions.
3. **No free AIS with verified coverage of Vietnamese waters was found.** GFW is noncommercial and about 5 days behind. Sentinel-1C/1D onboard AIS is restricted and reported to be downlinked over European waters only (UNVERIFIED). aisstream.io is free but publishes no terms, and users report gaps, including one report of no AIS around Vietnam in July 2026. Commercial satellite AIS (Spire and exactEarth, now inside Kpler; ORBCOMM) is paid and no price is public. Open AIS archives with clear licences exist only outside Asia (NOAA, Denmark, Norway, Finland) and fit validation, not Vietnam.
4. **In Vietnam the compliance record is VMS, not AIS.** Fishing vessels of 15 m and longer must carry VMS (about 99% compliance reported for a mid-year date, probably 2024), while AIS carriage rules do not reach most fishing boats. VMS data sit with the fisheries authority and the device providers (Viettel's S-Tracking is one). An AIS-only dark flag will mark many lawful, VMS-tracked boats. Matching to VMS where access exists is the stronger test. The EU yellow card was still in force in mid-August 2026. All UNVERIFIED (search snippets).
5. **Noncommercial only; keep out of any Viettel-facing path.** GFW APIs and downloads (CC BY-NC 4.0; GFW repo licence read), the GFW Nature-paper code and data (Apache-2.0 plus a noncommercial notice), SARDet-100K (CC BY-NC 4.0, read), ALOS-2 StripMap L2.1 (noncommercial, read), and xView3-SAR, OSSDD and NASTaR (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 reported, UNVERIFIED). Licence unclear, so treat as restricted until cleared: SSDD, HRSID, LS-SSDD, SAR-Ship-Dataset, SRSDD, FUSAR-Ship, OpenSARShip, the SARFish labels (the SARFish imagery itself is CC BY-SA 3.0 IGO), RCM, VIIRS Boat Detection.
6. **The only commercially clean Sentinel-1 vessel labels found are the Skylight labels, and they fit the transfer question better than the AOIs.** 61,655 Sentinel-1 point labels, 98% from S1A scenes dated 2020 to 2022. 10,364 fall inside the South China Sea AOI, 4 inside the Ca Mau box and none inside the Gulf of Tonkin box. The 17,582 attribute labels are dominated by large vessels (median 115 m; 54 under 15 m), so they say little about the small boats that decide recall by length.
7. **Not available for the January 2027 demo:** Sentinel-1A (retired), LOTUSat-1 (launch aimed at 2027, UNVERIFIED), Viettel satellites (research only, as reported), open X-band scenes over the sub-areas (ICEYE 1 scene in the Gulf of Tonkin box, Capella 4 items from one collect, Umbra 0, none in Ca Mau), and NISAR over ocean (L-band data public since 20 July 2026 according to search snippets, ocean value unknown).
8. **Blocked hosts limit verification.** CDSE APIs, Planetary Computer, GFW, ASF, xView3, Hugging Face, arXiv, Nature and most regulators are blocked from this environment. Every claim that depends on them is marked UNVERIFIED (search snippet). The Open items section says what to open and why.

**Sentinel-1 constellation status**

- **Sentinel-1A.** Launched 3 April 2014. The last IW GRD products in the AWS bucket are dated 2026-06-29 (442 products that day); there are none on 2026-06-30 or later (VERIFIED, S3 listing). The ASF documentation states that Sentinel-1A would stop collecting data on 29 June 2026 (VERIFIED-SECONDARY: ASFHyP3/hyp3-docs, `docs/sentinel1.md`, commit 762bae6 of 2026-09-04). The brief and search snippets give 30 June 2026 as the end of operations (UNVERIFIED search snippet: https://dataspace.copernicus.eu/node/4026, https://www.esa.int/Applications/Observing_the_Earth/Copernicus/Time_to_say_goodbye_to_Sentinel-1A, https://defence-industry-space.ec.europa.eu/sentinel-1a-concludes-its-mission-leaving-lasting-legacy-copernicus-2026-06-30_en).
- **Sentinel-1B.** Launched 25 April 2016; no data after 23 December 2021 because of a power-supply anomaly, mission end declared July 2022 (CDSE docs and ASF docs, read).
- **Sentinel-1C.** Launched 5 December 2024 (CDSE docs, read); regular acquisitions since 26 March 2025 (ASF docs). It was manoeuvred in June 2026 and acquired no data for about two weeks (ASF docs; search snippets give 9 to 23 June: https://dataspace.copernicus.eu/node/3949, https://ospo.noaa.gov/data/messages/2026/06/MSG_20260609_1900.html). The bucket agrees: no S1C IW product on 2026-06-10, 164 on 2026-06-24, 431 on 2026-06-26 (VERIFIED).
- **Sentinel-1D.** Launched 4 November 2025; calibrated data available from 17 April 2026 (ASF docs). The bucket agrees: 3 S1D IW products on 2026-04-16 and 394 on 2026-04-18 (VERIFIED). It is positioned one day behind the original Sentinel-1C (former 1B) orbit.
- **Revisit.** Each satellite has a 12-day repeat cycle; two satellites offset give 6 days. Most landmasses are imaged every 12 days and select areas of interest every 6 days; the 1C and 1D acquisition plan is shifted by one day against the old 1A and 1B plan (ASF docs, VERIFIED-SECONDARY).
- The AWS bucket sentinel-s1-l1c holds GRD only (root listing shows only the GRD/ prefix). SLC is available through CDSE and ASF.

**Terms and sovereignty.** The Copernicus Sentinel Data Legal Notice grants free access for reproduction, distribution, communication to the public and adaptation, requires the credit lines 'Copernicus Sentinel data [Year]' and 'Contains modified Copernicus Sentinel data [Year]', and allows access to be limited in rare cases of security concerns, protection of third-party rights or risk of service disruption (VERIFIED-SECONDARY: a Markdown rendering of the notice at https://github.com/chapmanjacobd/satmaps/blob/main/LICENSE.md; the official text at https://sentinels.copernicus.eu/documents/247904/690755/Sentinel_Data_Legal_Notice was not opened). Reproduction is on that list, so a local mirror of the needed scenes is allowed in principle (inference). The CDSE FAQ confirms there is no difference between EU and non-EU users (read). Earth Engine and Planetary Computer are US-operated cloud services and are not sovereign; the AWS bucket is hosted in Frankfurt (eu-central-1) and managed by Element 84 (registry); CDSE is the EU-operated service.

**Restricted high-resolution data.** CDSE lists Copernicus Contributing Mission data (for example the PAZ radar satellite) and states that access to very-high-resolution data is restricted to eligible user categories (FAQ, read). Whether a Vietnamese operator is eligible is UNKNOWN.

**What "dark" means in this region**

- GFW's own documentation gives the same reasons for unmatched SAR detections: not all vessels are required to use AIS; coastal blind spots from restricted satellite access and from poor reception in dense traffic and with low-quality devices; transponders switched off. It adds that not every vessel that turns off its transponder is engaging in suspicious or illicit activity (VERIFIED: gfwr vignette "Introduction to SAR vessel detections", https://github.com/GlobalFishingWatch/gfwr/blob/main/vignettes/articles/sar_vessel_detections.Rmd).
- AIS is mandatory for large and international ships, and most small fishing boats are exempt (SOLAS V/19, UNVERIFIED). In Vietnam the duty that reaches fishing vessels of 15 m and longer is VMS, a separate satellite stream held by the authority and device providers (UNVERIFIED). A SAR detection with no AIS match is therefore expected for most small Vietnamese fishing boats, whatever their legal status.
- Published context: 72 to 76% of industrial fishing vessels are not publicly tracked, much of that around South Asia, Southeast Asia and Africa (Paolo et al. 2024; UNVERIFIED search snippets from scholars.duke.edu, e360.yale.edu and PMC).
- Satellite AIS performs poorly in dense traffic because of message collisions, and a 2020 review lists South-East Asia among the poor-detection areas (UNVERIFIED search snippet: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2011.11304). Matching SAR to AIS also depends on ship density and time lag, and moving ships are displaced along track in SAR (UNVERIFIED search snippets; the repo's `src/darkvessel/ais/match.py` already models the shift).
- Recommendation (judgement): label unmatched results "no AIS match", add "no VMS match" only where VMS data are actually matched, and never use the word illegal.

### Methods behind the VERIFIED statements

- **S3 reads.** Anonymous `curl` listings and range reads of the public buckets named in the tables. Latency (A01): for the last 20 scenes in `data/s1_scenes.csv` (29 Aug to 29 Sep 2026), the difference between the product stop time and the S3 `LastModified` time of the product files (all 27 objects share one value); minimum 2.1 h, median 3.5 h, maximum 8.2 h. `LastModified` could be reset by a re-upload, so treat it as an upper bound on first availability.
- **Platform counts per day (A01).** Listing of `GRD/{year}/{month}/{day}/{mode}/{pol}/` and counting product-name prefixes: 2026-09-28 IW S1C 433, S1D 540; 2026-09-29 S1C 442, S1D 467; 2026-09-20 S1C 404, S1D 510; 2026-06-20 S1A 558, S1D 433, S1C 0; 2026-06-29 S1A 442, S1C 429, S1D 503; 2026-06-30 S1C 456, S1D 459 (no S1A); 2026-06-10 S1A 542, S1D 393 (no S1C); 2026-04-16 S1A 502, S1C 454, S1D 3; 2026-04-18 S1A 537, S1C 407, S1D 394.
- **Gulf of Tonkin scene search (A01).** A scratch re-implementation, in pure Python with no repo writes, of the logic in `src/darkvessel/s1/aws.py` (day listing, time-of-day pre-filter by longitude, footprint test on `productInfo.json`, S1C and S1D only, IW with DV or SV polarisation). It tests overlap with the bounding box 105.6 to 108.2 E, 19.0 to 21.6 N, not with a polygon. Run on the Ca Mau box it reproduced the session's result exactly (50 products, all S1D, 30 ascending and 20 descending, relative orbits 18, 26, 99, 91 with 16, 16, 14, 4 products, 27 dates, median gap 4 days, best overlap 42%), which validates the method. The script is not in the repo.
- **Skylight database (D01).** `data/metadata.sqlite3` queried read-only after a shallow clone (commit 60b003d). Pixel coordinates convert to longitude and latitude with the Web Mercator grid at zoom 13 and 512 px tiles (world width 4,194,304 px); this reproduces the scene bounds stored in the images table. Region counts use the Ca Mau and Gulf of Tonkin boxes from `src/darkvessel/config.py` and the polygon in `data/aoi.gpkg`, opened read-only and tested with an even-odd point-in-polygon rule.
- **Open X-band catalogs (A12 to A14).** Breadth-first crawls of the static STAC trees on 2026-10-02 (ICEYE collection iceye-sar.json; Umbra stac/catalog.json; Capella by-datetime tree and the maritime use-case collection). Centroids or bounding-box centres were tested against the AOIs. For Capella, item counts matched the catalog link counts on sample days.
- **Satlas points (B08).** `latest.geojson` downloaded (6.9 MB) and points counted against the two boxes and the AOI polygon.
- **Registry and documentation clones.** Shallow clones, read only: awslabs/open-data-registry 0046286 (2026-10-01); eu-cdse/documentation a73e234 (2026-10-01); google/earthengine-catalog a16446d (2026-09-28); asfadmin/Discovery-asf_search 0a31fbb (2026-09-24); ASFHyP3/hyp3-docs 762bae6 (2026-09-04); GlobalFishingWatch/gfwr and gfw-api-python-client (default branches, 2026-10-02); DIUx-xView/SARFish and DIUx-xView/xview3-reference (raw files); allenai/vessel-detection-sentinels 60b003d (2024-05-10); GlobalFishingWatch/paper-industrial-activity c5fd585 (2024-03-20); the README and LICENSE files of the dataset repositories were fetched from raw.githubusercontent.com.
