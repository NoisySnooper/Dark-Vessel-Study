# Additional data sources for the dark-vessel pipeline (South China Sea AOI)

> **"Dark" does not mean illegal.** It only means that no AIS position was matched to a radar detection. Many vessels are not required to carry AIS, AIS can be off for lawful reasons, and satellite AIS misses messages in busy coastal waters. A lit boat seen at night with no AIS match is a lead for review, not evidence of wrongdoing.

Date: 2026-10-02 (UTC). Companion file: `data/data_additions.csv` (one row per source; ids N = night lights, W = weather and sea state, O = optical, S = other SAR, V = Vietnamese). This document adds to `docs/data_landscape.md`. It repeats nothing from there except row B03 (VIIRS Boat Detection), whose status is upgraded, and A10 (ALOS-4), which gets a 2026 update.

Status labels follow `docs/data_landscape.md`: VERIFIED (method named), VERIFIED-SECONDARY (third-party text read in full), UNVERIFIED (search snippet; the page could not be opened), UNKNOWN. "Measured" means a number computed in this session from S3 object listings or downloaded files. All probes and reads are dated 2026-10-02. "Blocked" means the proxy answered HTTP 403 to the CONNECT request (curl reports HTTP 000); those hosts are listed at the end.

## Summary

1. **Night lights are the one addition that changes the coverage picture.** The raw VIIRS Day/Night Band (DNB) SDR and geolocation files of Suomi NPP, NOAA-20 and NOAA-21 sit on three public NOAA buckets with anonymous access. On the night of 2 October 2026 the three satellites crossed the AOI at 17:40, 18:21 to 18:27 and 19:01 to 19:04 UTC with nadir tracks near 120E, 107E and 100E, and each granule spans about 30 degrees of longitude, so the whole AOI, including the central sea that Sentinel-1 never imaged in 90 days, is covered every night. Upload lag measured today: median 31 to 32 minutes for NOAA-20 and NOAA-21, 65 minutes for Suomi NPP. The data are NOAA open data (attribution requested). Limits: only lit boats are seen, cloud hides them, the pixel is 750 m, and moonlight raises the background. A lit-boat detector must be written in-house: EOG's ready-made Boat Detection product sits on a blocked host and its automated access is reported to be a paid tier.
2. **Weather and sea-state context can be added today at zero cost.** GFS 0.25 degree (10 m wind, gust, rain rate, wave height; analysis files appear 3.5 to 3.8 hours after the cycle time), ECMWF open data (CC BY 4.0; 6.5 to 7.6 hours), Himawari-9 full-disk imagery every 10 minutes (10 to 12 minutes behind) with NOAA cloud masks (18 minutes), and GPM IMERG rain through a CC BY 4.0 Zarr mirror. The era5-pds bucket is dead to anonymous users (HTTP 403); Google's ARCO-ERA5 store runs to 2026-09-26 (ERA5T) and NCAR's bucket to June 2026. These layers explain the rain-cell and wind clutter seen in the regional run and give every detection a wind and wave attribute.
3. **Optical confirmation gains little.** Landsat 9 exists since 2021, but the AWS bucket is requester-pays, the Google public bucket has no Landsat 9 folder, and the USGS hosts are blocked. Sentinel-3 is too coarse (300 m and 1 km). Among the sources checked, only VIIRS DNB images the AOI at night and is open. SDGSAT-1 (10 m night imagery) is free for research after registration and approval, Luojia-1 is decommissioned, and Jilin-1 night imagery is tasked and paid.
4. **No other SAR can be added now.** SAOCOM, KOMPSAT-5 and ALOS-4 are paid outside their home programmes; no access path for foreign users was found for Gaofen-3; NovaSAR-1 and TerraSAR-X/PAZ are research-only under proposal; Sentinel Asia supplies data only for declared disasters.
5. **Vietnamese sources.** VNREDSat-1 still returns images in its 14th year, but access is by agreement with VNSC. The Vietnam Data Cube duplicates open data. The full text of Decree 41/2026/ND-CP on VMS is hosted on two provincial servers (blocked here); the snippet of its Article 26 confirms that VMS data stay in the state system, so nothing opens them to a pipeline.
6. **For the Viettel-facing path** the clean additions are the NOAA JPSS buckets, GFS, ECMWF open data, Himawari-9, IMERG, Black Marble, Light Every Night and ERA5 (Copernicus licence; two registry texts describe it as allowing commercial use with credit; the licence PDF itself was not opened). Keep SDGSAT-1, NovaSAR-1, TerraSAR-X/PAZ science data and anything under an unread licence (EOG VBD) out of that path until the terms are read.

Notes on N01, from the files read:

- The NOAA-20 granule of 17:42 UTC was flagged Night, nadir 116.8 to 117.9E and 0.3 to 5.2N. Over its sea pixels the median radiance was 7.3e-10 W cm-2 sr-1, the 99.9th percentile 1.0e-8 and the maximum 2.2e-6, so lit targets stand out from the background by two to three orders of magnitude. The geolocation file carries lunar zenith and azimuth, moon illumination fraction (58.9 percent that night) and phase angle, which a detector needs for moonlight and glint handling.
- Volume is small. The AOI night pass is 4 to 5 granules per satellite, so about 15 SDR and geolocation pairs, or about 1 GB, per night for the constellation.
- Retention: the registry states that from 14 September 2026 a subset of JPSS products on AWS is kept for 90 days; VIIRS-DNB-SDR and VIIRS-DNB-GEO are on the list kept without a retention policy. NCC imagery and the cloud mask are not on that list, hold 2026 data only, and must be mirrored within 90 days.
- No detector is supplied. The reference design is EOG's VBD algorithm (Elvidge et al. 2015, an anchor paper in `docs/bibliometrics.md`): spike detection against the local background, moonlit and lightning filters, a cloud mask. A first version can be built on N01 and N02 alone. The upgrade of B03 is limited: registration and CC BY 4.0 move from UNVERIFIED to VERIFIED-SECONDARY, and one third party reports a paid tier for automated access; the licence PDF is still unread.
- EOG's monthly and annual DNB composites in Earth Engine carry a public-domain notice in the catalog (NOAA_VIIRS_DNB_MONTHLY_V1_VCMSLCFG); they are not boat detections but can mask persistent offshore lights (VERIFIED, earthengine-catalog d846f23).

## Satlas (B08) and the fixed-structure class

Row B08 already lists 4,666 Satlas platform and turbine points inside the AOI (snapshot of 2026-07-27, ODC-BY). They are the only independent check available for the 25,224 fixed structures of the regional run: match the project's fixed class to Satlas points within a buffer to estimate its precision on platforms and turbines, and count Satlas points with no fixed detection to estimate its recall. The Shanwei wind farm, where the persistence test missed about 800 turbine returns, is the first test case. Satlas points are model predictions with scores, not ground truth, so disagreements need a quicklook.
